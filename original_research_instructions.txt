ORIGINAL RESEARCH INSTRUCTIONS
English Summary of the Original Research Direction and Tasks

Source: Original Chinese research instructions titled "项目方向与当前任务说明" (Research Direction and Current Tasks).

This document summarizes the original research instructions provided to the research team. It is an English summary, not a verbatim translation or a retrospectively developed experimental protocol.

1. RESEARCH OBJECTIVE

Investigate how large language models make clinical decisions when presented with conflicting medical evidence.

The central research question is whether AI-generated clinical recommendations exhibit different levels of stability across a hierarchy of decisions, particularly between general clinical principles and specific operational recommendations.

2. THREE LEVELS OF CLINICAL DECISION-MAKING

The research was organized around three decision levels:

Level 1 — Policy Decision:
The overall clinical principle or recommendation.

Level 2 — Operational Decision:
Specific implementation parameters, such as the recommended interval for repositioning patients.

Level 3 — Evidence Attribution:
The clinical evidence prioritized by the model when formulating its recommendation.

The purpose was to examine decision stability across these three levels.

3. EXPERIMENT E1: CONFLICTING CLINICAL EVIDENCE

The research team was instructed to review relevant clinical literature and construct approximately 10–15 candidate evidence packs containing conflicting recommendations.

Each evidence pack should preferably include:

- A high-quality randomized controlled trial.
- A clinical guideline or systematic review.
- An observational or real-world clinical study.

The selected evidence should demonstrate meaningful differences or tensions in clinical recommendations.

The objective was to create controlled experimental scenarios in which AI models must interpret and reconcile conflicting clinical evidence.

4. EXPERIMENT E2: STANDARDIZED CLINICAL QUESTIONS

The research team was also instructed to prepare standardized clinical case questions for comparison with the conflicting-evidence experiments.

These questions were intended to examine model decision behavior in a separate, more standardized clinical setting.

5. LITERATURE SCREENING AND EVIDENCE ANNOTATION

During literature preparation, the research team was instructed to:

- Identify relevant clinical publications.
- Verify study types and clinical contexts.
- Extract key recommendations and supporting evidence.
- Identify genuine differences between clinical recommendations.
- Apply directional evidence labels (+1, -1, 0) consistently.

These directional labels were used to characterize the literature during evidence preparation. They were not model-generated clinical decisions.

6. RELATIONSHIP TO THE FINAL EXPERIMENTS

The original instructions proposed approximately 10–15 candidate conflicting-evidence packs. Nine evidence packs were ultimately included in the finalized E1 experiments.

The original research framework specified three hierarchical decision levels: policy, operational implementation, and evidence attribution.

It did not prescribe a separate model-generated field named Operational_Strategy.

The final experiments and statistical analyses are documented separately in the manuscript and the methodological transparency materials.

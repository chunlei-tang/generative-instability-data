METHODOLOGICAL TRANSPARENCY AND REPRODUCIBILITY

Decision Stability under Conflicting Evidence in AI-Assisted Clinical Decision Systems

1. PURPOSE

This document provides additional methodological clarification regarding the experimental procedures, model-generated outputs, analytical variables, entropy calculations, and reproducibility of the study.

The purpose is to distinguish the decisions generated directly by the AI models from the analytical representations used to evaluate decision stability.

2. EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN

The study was conducted in two separate stages.

The initial exploratory stage involved seven repeated runs using earlier model versions. These experiments informed the development and interpretation of the subsequent study.

The revised experimental stage involved 20 repeated runs using updated ChatGPT and Gemini models. These 20-run experiments constitute the primary basis of the reported statistical analyses.

The two stages were conducted separately using different model versions. They should not be interpreted as 27 repeated runs of a single experiment or as directly interchangeable experimental datasets.

The primary experiments included:

E1 Baseline:
Clinical decision-making under structured conflicting evidence.

E1 Intervention:
Clinical decision-making using the corresponding intervention prompts.

E2 Baseline:
Decision-making on standardized clinical questions.

The E1 experiments used nine finalized evidence packs. The E2 experiments used 50 standardized questions.

3. MODEL-GENERATED DECISION FIELDS

The experimental prompts requested three categorical decision fields:

- Policy_Decision
- Operational_Interval
- Evidence_Priority

A verbalized Confidence score was also recorded.

These fields were generated directly by the models and preserved in the experimental JSON outputs.

The researchers did not manually assign individual model decisions.

Operational_Strategy was not requested as a separate model-generated field.

4. ANALYTICAL REPRESENTATION OF OPERATIONAL DECISIONS

The model-generated Operational_Interval responses were examined using two complementary analytical representations.

A. Operational_Strategy (K = 3)

This representation distinguishes three operational approaches:

- Fixed Interval
- Risk-based
- NotSpecified

The numerical interval responses 2h, 3h, and 4h are grouped under Fixed Interval.

B. Operational_Interval (K = 4)

This representation distinguishes four analytical categories:

- 2h
- 3h
- 4h
- No Fixed Interval

The original responses Risk-based and NotSpecified are grouped under No Fixed Interval because neither specifies a numerical repositioning interval.

The complete mapping is:

Original response: 2h
Operational_Strategy: Fixed Interval
Analytical Operational_Interval: 2h

Original response: 3h
Operational_Strategy: Fixed Interval
Analytical Operational_Interval: 3h

Original response: 4h
Operational_Strategy: Fixed Interval
Analytical Operational_Interval: 4h

Original response: Risk-based
Operational_Strategy: Risk-based
Analytical Operational_Interval: No Fixed Interval

Original response: NotSpecified
Operational_Strategy: NotSpecified
Analytical Operational_Interval: No Fixed Interval

These representations are deterministic transformations of the model-generated operational responses, rather than additional decisions generated independently by the models.

5. ENTROPY CALCULATIONS

Decision stability was evaluated using Shannon entropy:

H(X) = -sum[p(i) * log2(p(i))]

where p(i) is the observed proportion of responses in category i.

Normalized entropy was calculated as:

H_normalized(X) = H(X) / log2(K)

where K is the number of categories in the corresponding analytical representation.

For Operational_Strategy, K = 3.

For the analytical Operational_Interval measure, K = 4.

Although the original prompt allowed five operational response options, the analytical Operational_Interval representation contains four categories because Risk-based and NotSpecified are combined.

The value of K therefore refers to the analytical category structure, not necessarily the number of options in the original model prompt.

6. VERIFICATION AGAINST ARCHIVED MODEL OUTPUTS

The operational entropy values were independently recalculated from the archived 20-run model-generated JSON outputs using the category mappings described above.

The following values were reproduced:

Gemini E1 Baseline:
Operational_Strategy = 0.5999 bits
Operational_Interval = 0.3164 bits

Gemini E1 Intervention:
Operational_Strategy = 0.7373 bits
Operational_Interval = 0.4773 bits

ChatGPT E1 Baseline:
Operational_Strategy = 0.0521 bits
Operational_Interval = 0.0521 bits

ChatGPT E1 Intervention:
Operational_Strategy = 0.0000 bits
Operational_Interval = 0.0000 bits

These recalculations demonstrate that the reported operational entropy values can be reproduced from the archived model outputs using the stated mappings.

The mappings were identified through independent verification of the archived data. Numerical agreement alone does not establish when these mappings were originally adopted; any historical claims about the original analysis workflow require separate documentation.

7. REPRODUCIBILITY MATERIALS

The study repository provides experimental prompts, structured evidence materials, and model-output documentation.

For independent computational verification, the complete archived 20-run outputs and corresponding statistical result tables should be provided alongside the documented category mappings.

Experimental replication involves submitting the same prompts to the specified model versions under comparable settings.

Computational reproducibility involves recalculating the reported statistics from the archived model outputs.

Because generative AI models may produce variable responses and model versions may change over time, a newly conducted experiment is not expected to reproduce every original model response exactly.

The archived outputs provide the appropriate basis for verifying the numerical results reported in the manuscript.

8. INTERPRETATION

The study evaluates decision stability across three hierarchical clinical decision levels:

Level 1: Policy decisions

Level 2: Operational decisions

Level 3: Evidence prioritization

The four reported analytical measures do not imply that the models generated four independent categorical decision fields.

Instead, the operational level was examined through two complementary representations of the same model-generated Operational_Interval response.

This distinction clarifies the relationship between the experimental prompts, raw JSON outputs, and reported entropy calculations.
